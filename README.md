# astro-data-repo

The night sky, as data for the oceansensing.org ocean map: the stars to
magnitude 9 with their names, the constellations, the Milky Way's outline, the
NGC and IC objects and the nebulae, the brightest sources each band of the
spectrum sees and the pulsars, galaxies with their redshifts, the comets, the
International Space Station's path, the major meteor showers, Earth
satellites' orbital elements, whole-sky pictures across the spectrum, and maps
and photographs of the Moon, the Sun, the planets and their great moons.

Updated every two hours: the satellites each time, the comets, the station's
path and the meteor list daily, the catalogs weekly.

## Sources and credits

The stars: the Yale Bright Star Catalogue, 5th revised edition (Hoffleit & Warren 1991; NASA's Astronomical Data Center, CDS catalog V/50), which states no license. The names: the IAU Catalog of Star Names (the IAU Working Group on Star Names, 2022-04-04), released under Creative Commons Attribution by its own header. The constellations' figures and names and the Milky Way's outline: d3-celestial (Olaf Frohn, BSD license). Its readme names their upstreams: the figures are the IAU and Sky & Telescope's constellation charts (Roger Sinnott, Rick Fienberg, Alan MacRobert's patterns), released under Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 and credited; the Milky Way is J. R. Vieira's Milky Way Outline Catalog, credited.

The fainter stars: the PPM Star Catalogue (Röser & Bastian 1991; Bastian & Röser 1993; Astronomisches Rechen-Institut Heidelberg; CDS I/146, I/193, I/206 and I/208) and the Henry Draper Catalogue (Cannon & Pickering 1918–1924; CDS III/135A), through CDS; neither states a license, and each is credited. Their estimated magnitudes are set to Tycho-2's star counts (ESA; CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO), counted through NASA's HEASARC, credited in the bands' `source`.

The NGC and IC objects, the Messier objects among them: OpenNGC (Mattia Verga), released under CC BY-SA 4.0 — and so are `ngc.json` and `deepsky.json`, each saying so; its values from NED, HyperLEDA, SIMBAD, HEASARC's tables and Harold Corwin's notes, each credited; the four objects its addendum names by their Caldwell numbers go by another designation or their name, the Caldwell list carrying a stated copyright. Their names and Wikipedia articles: Wikidata, CC0 by its own licensing page. The nebulae: Barnard's catalogue of dark objects (CDS VII/220A), Lynds' dark (VII/7A) and bright (VII/9) nebulae, and Sharpless's H II regions (VII/20), through CDS VizieR; none states a license, and each is credited.

The band sources: NASA's HEASARC tables — Fermi LAT's 4FGL-DR4 and its pulsars (2PC), Swift-BAT's 157 months, ROSAT's 2RXS, the 2MASS Redshift Survey's galaxies, IRAS's point sources, Milliquas's quasars and blazars and NVSS's radio sources; the Fermi, Swift and IRAS catalogs are NASA's, the 2MASS Redshift Survey (Huchra et al. 2012) is built on the 2MASS Extended Source Catalog (IPAC: no usage restrictions), and ROSAT's 2RXS, Milliquas (E. Flesch) and NVSS (NRAO) state no license of their own — each credited by name in the file; RACS-mid (Duchesne et al. 2024), CSIRO's, through its CASDA, released under CC BY 4.0 by its collection in CSIRO's Data Access Portal. The pulsars: the ATNF Pulsar Catalogue (PSRCAT v2 from CSIRO's Data Access Portal, BSD-style, its license text carried in the catalog's `notice`). The galaxies: every 2MASS Redshift Survey galaxy with a redshift (Huchra et al. 2012, through HEASARC) and the Sloan Digital Sky Survey's spectroscopic galaxies (DR18, its SkyServer; SDSS's released data in the public domain).

The comets: the IAU Minor Planet Center's `CometEls.txt`, which asks for its credit. The station's path: NASA Johnson Space Center's ISS Trajectory Operations and Planning office's published ephemeris, a work of the US government. The meteor showers: their radiants, dates, speeds and parents from the IAU Meteor Data Center's list of established showers, which states no license and asks to be cited (Jenniskens et al. 2020, Planetary and Space Science 182, 104821; Jopek & Kaňuchová 2017, Planetary and Space Science 143, 3); their rates from the International Meteor Organization's 2027 Meteor Shower Calendar (J. Rendtel, ed.), its Working List of Visual Meteor Showers, credited; the calendar states no license. The satellites: CelesTrak's GP data (celestrak.org); the elements are the US Space Force's (18th Space Defense Squadron) through Space-Track.org, whose documentation gives USSPACECOM's express blanket approval to redistribute basic SSA data — element sets and OMMs among them — conditioned on citation.

The sky across the spectrum, one whole-sky picture a band: gamma rays (Fermi LAT's 15 years, NASA SVS), hard X-rays (Swift-BAT's 70 months), X-rays (the ROSAT All-Sky Survey's diffuse maps, ¼, ¾ and 1½ keV), ultraviolet (GALEX's diffuse far UV, Murthy 2014, MAST), visible (ESA/Gaia/DPAC's EDR3 colour sky, A. Moitinho — **CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO**, and so is `visible-v1.jpg`), H-alpha (Finkbeiner 2003), near infrared (COBE DIRBE's zodi-subtracted mission averages at 1.25, 2.2 and 3.5 µm, Hauser et al. 1998, NASA, through LAMBDA), mid and far infrared (IRIS, 12 and 100 µm), the cosmic microwave background and 23 GHz (WMAP), 21 cm (the LAB HI Survey) and 408 MHz (Haslam et al. 1982), from each mission's own files or NASA's SkyView — named with their credits and licenses by `spectrum/index.json`.

The Moon's map: NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio's CGI Moon Kit color map, adapted from the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter camera's Hapke-normalized WAC mosaic (a PDS product, public domain), named with its credit, license, source and SHA-256 by `bodies/index.json`. The photographs of the Sun, Venus, the Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto and Titan: from NOAA's GOES-19 SUVI, ESA's Solar Orbiter and Rosetta (CC BY-SA 3.0 IGO), NSO (CC BY 4.0), Hubble and Webb (CC BY 4.0), Mariner 10, Magellan, Apollo 17, Suomi NPP, Viking, Cassini, Voyager 2, Galileo and Juno (public domain, or CC BY and CC BY-SA where a processor's license says so, CC0 for one), each named under `photos` in `bodies/index.json` with who took it and when, what its colors are, its credit as its page asks, its license and that license's page. A public-domain picture's burned-in caption is cut away; a licensed one's attribution is kept.
